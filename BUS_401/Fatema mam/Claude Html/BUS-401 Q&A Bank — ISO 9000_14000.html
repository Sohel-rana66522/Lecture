01 ISO 9001

Q1. What is ISO 9001?

ISO 9001 is the international standard that specifies requirements for a Quality Management System (QMS). Organizations use it to demonstrate their ability to consistently provide products and services that meet customer and regulatory requirements. It is the most popular standard in the ISO 9000 series and the only standard in the series to which any organization can actually certify.

Q2. When was ISO 9001 first published, and what is the current version?

ISO 9001 was first published in 1987 by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), an international agency composed of the national standards bodies of more than 160 countries. The current version of ISO 9001 was released in May 2026.

Q3. To whom does ISO 9001:2026 apply, and how widely is it used?

It applies to any organization, regardless of size or industry. More than one million organizations from more than 160 countries have applied the ISO 9001 standard requirements to their quality management systems.

Q4. How does using the ISO 9001 standard help organizations?

  • Organize processes
  • Improve the efficiency of processes
  • Continually improve

02 What Topics Does ISO 9001 Cover?

Q5. What topics does ISO 9001 cover?

ISO 9001 is based on the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) methodology and provides a process-oriented approach to documenting and reviewing the structure, responsibilities, and procedures required to achieve effective quality management in an organization. Specific sections cover:

  1. Requirements for a quality management system, including documented information, planning, and determining process interactions
  2. Responsibilities of management
  3. Management of resources, including human resources and an organization's work environment
  4. Product realization, including the steps from design to delivery
  5. Measurement, analysis, and improvement of the QMS through activities like internal audits and corrective/preventive action

03 ISO 9001 Requirements — Clause 7 (Product Realization)

Q6. Outline Clause 7 (Product Realization) of the ISO 9001 requirements.

  • 7.1 Planning of product realization
  • 7.2 Customer-related processes
    • 7.2.1 Determination of requirements related to the product
    • 7.2.2 Review of requirements related to the product
    • 7.2.3 Customer communication
  • 7.3 Design and development
    • 7.3.1 Design and development planning
    • 7.3.2 Design and development inputs
    • 7.3.3 Design and development outputs
    • 7.3.4 Design and development review
    • 7.3.5 Design and development verification
    • 7.3.6 Design and development validation
    • 7.3.7 Control of design and development changes
  • 7.4 Purchasing
    • 7.4.1 Purchasing process
    • 7.4.2 Purchasing information
    • 7.4.3 Verification of purchased product
  • 7.5 Production and service provision
    • 7.5.1 Control of production and service provision
    • 7.5.2 Validation of processes for production and service provision
    • 7.5.3 Identification and traceability
    • 7.5.4 Customer property
    • 7.5.5 Preservation of product
  • 7.6 Control of monitoring and measuring equipment

Q7. Why does Clause 7.3 (Design and Development) include review, verification, and validation as separate sub-clauses?

Each checks a different thing: review evaluates the design's ability to meet requirements at planned stages, verification confirms the design outputs meet the design inputs, and validation confirms the resulting product meets requirements for its intended/specified use. Keeping them separate ensures each check happens deliberately rather than being assumed by the others.

04 Review — May 2026 (Direction of the Revised Standard)

Q8. What are the key focus areas of the May 2026 review of ISO 9001?

  1. Ethics & Quality Culture — leadership accountability goes beyond processes to building a culture of integrity and trust.
  2. Risk & Opportunity Clarity — stronger guidance to separate and manage risk and opportunity effectively.
  3. Climate Change & Stakeholder Focus — organizations must integrate sustainability and environmental risks into their QMS.
  4. Digital Transformation — recognition of AI, IoT, and Industry 4.0 in quality systems.
  5. Alignment with Other Standards — easier integration with ISO 14001, ISO 27001, and ISO 45001.

05 Environmental Management System (EMS)

Q9. What is an EMS?

An EMS is a systematic way of managing an organization's environmental affairs.

  • Based on the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) model
  • Focused on continual improvement of the system
  • Addresses the immediate and long-term impact of an organization's products, services and processes on the environment
  • A tool to improve environmental performance

Q10. What characteristics define an effective EMS?

  • Focuses not only on what is done but also why
  • A top management priority
  • Dynamic
  • Flexible and simple
  • Relies on employee involvement
  • Endorses continual improvement

Q11. What should organizations keep in mind when building or using an EMS?

  • Much of an EMS may already be in place within the organization
  • The key is using a systematic approach to planning, controlling, and improving environmental efforts
  • It can be a vehicle for positive change

Q12. How should an EMS audit be designed, and what areas does it typically cover?

Each EMS audit should be designed to fit that specific company's legal requirements and environmental interactions, working closely with the company's team and providing clear, honest communication from the start. The audit's purview is broad, covering:

  • Drinking water
  • Storm water
  • Wastewater
  • Waste disposal
  • Storage and disposal of hazardous chemicals
  • Tanks, both above ground and underground
  • Air emissions
  • Emergency response plans

Q13. What documents and topics does EMS auditing typically review?

  • Presence of a written EMS manual
  • Organizational charts and position descriptions
  • Corporate environmental procedures and policies
  • Training requirements
  • External and internal communication plans
  • Operational and maintenance budgets
  • EMS internal verification procedures

The internal audit of an EMS is meant to be straightforward and easy to understand.

06 ISO 10000 Series

Q14. What is the ISO 10000 series?

The ISO 10000 series is a collection of international standards and guidelines created by the International Organization for Standardization. It focuses on managing customer satisfaction, handling complaints, resolving disputes, and providing supportive guidance for quality management systems.

07 ISO 45001

Q15. What is ISO 45001?

ISO 45001 is an international standard that specifies requirements for an Occupational Health and Safety (OH&S) management system. It provides a framework for organizations to manage risks and improve OH&S performance.

Q16. What criteria and elements does ISO 45001 establish?

It establishes criteria for OH&S policy, objectives, planning, implementation, operation, auditing, and review. Key elements include:

  • Leadership commitment
  • Worker participation
  • Hazard identification and risk assessment
  • Legal and regulatory compliance
  • Emergency planning
  • Incident investigation
  • Continual improvement

Q17. What methodology does ISO 45001 use, and to whom does it apply?

It utilizes the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) methodology to systematically manage health and safety risks. It applies to organizations of all sizes and can be integrated with other ISO management system standards.

Q18. Why is ISO 45001 important? (Value to organizations)

  • Provides an internationally-recognized framework for managing OH&S risks, enabling systematic hazard assessment and risk control, which reduces workplace injuries, illnesses, and incidents.
  • Shows employees and external stakeholders the organization's commitment to worker health, safety, and wellbeing — boosting reputation, morale, and retention.
  • Supports compliant risk management, helping lower insurance costs and ensuring legal/regulatory conformity.
  • Strengthens organizational resilience against safety threats and crises through required emergency preparedness and response protocols.
  • The PDCA framework allows the OH&S system to continually improve and evolve.

Q19. List the key benefits of ISO 45001.

  1. Framework to systematically manage OH&S risks
  2. Reduced workplace incidents and injuries
  3. Demonstrated commitment to worker health and safety
  4. Ensured compliance with OH&S regulations
  5. Increased organizational resilience
  6. Continual improvement of OH&S performance
